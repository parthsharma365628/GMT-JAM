# From Hell to Heaven

A single-player desktop browser game built with Three.js and Vite. One stage connects a third-person obstacle run to an isometric sword fight. The characters, hazards and scenery use basic geometry and flat materials.

## How a run works

1. Choose **Start Ascent**. A visual-novel cutscene plays first (click, Space, Enter or E to advance; Esc or Skip to jump ahead). Then the runner moves forward automatically. Steer with A/D or the arrow keys, jump gaps with Space, and avoid rocks and timed fire jets. Newer obstacles move: a spinning sweeper arm to jump, a sliding wall to step around, and boulders that roll down a lane toward the runner. V switches between third-person and first-person view, B toggles motion blur, M mutes the sound effects. A collision returns the player to the current segment's checkpoint.
2. Enter the dungeon. The camera rises, the entrance closes, and the Ash Warden approaches. Move with WASD, attack with J or left click, and dodge with K, Shift or right click. The guardian signals a heavy swing or ground slam before striking. Its recovery gives the player time to counterattack. Losing restarts the encounter.
3. Defeat the Warden, approach its golden sword and press E. Walk through the unlocked arch to begin the automatic ascent through Heaven. The victory screen shows the sword, elapsed time and retries. Escape pauses active gameplay.

## Where to make changes

- **Runner route generation:** `generateRoute` in `stage.js` builds a new random route each time a stage loads. It uses the stage's `segments` (number of obstacle segments, which sets the route length), `gapStart` (the first obstacle segment where gaps may appear), `obstacles` (each type with the sides it can spawn on), and the optional `weights` (how often a type is picked, default 1, split across its sides) and `minGap` (fewest segments between two of a type). Boulders use `weights: {boulder: .35}` and `minGap: {boulder: 6}`. Two consecutive segments never have the same type and side, so a type with only one side never appears twice in a row.
- **Moving obstacles (`sweeper`, `slider`, `boulder`):** built in `runner-obstacles.js` and created from `runner` in `world.js`, which stores the returned object on the hazard as `mover`. `run` in `main.js` calls `mover.update` each frame, `mover.hits` for collisions, `mover.near` for close calls and `mover.behind` (boulder only) to decide when the runner is past it; `resetRunner` calls `mover.reset`. The sweeper is a low spiked arm spinning around a centre post (`SWEEP_SPEED`, `SWEEP_R`, jump it). The slider is a 2.7-tall wall (jumpable at the top of a jump) that slides `SLIDE_RANGE` either side of centre. The boulder starts rolling when the runner is `TRIGGER` units away, from at least `START_AHEAD` units ahead, at `ROLL_SPEED`, and can be avoided by changing lanes or jumping. Its mesh lives in the track root and follows the route with `route.at`, so it rolls round turns. Add any of them to a stage's `obstacles` in `stage.js`.
- **Bloom:** edit `BLOOM` at the top of `post-fx.js`: `amount` (strength, 0 turns it off), `radius` (spread), `threshold` (how bright a pixel's brightest colour channel must be in linear light before it glows), `softness` (how gradually it fades in) and `realm` (per-realm overrides; Heaven uses a higher threshold so its white marble doesn't glow). It applies to the whole game. The bloom's high-pass filter is replaced to use the brightest channel instead of luminance, since luminance almost ignores red (Hell's lava cracks never bloomed). `main.js` calls `postFx.setRealm` in `load`. Before bloom, a clean pass drops NaN/infinite pixels and clamps highlights to 16, because a single bad pixel in the Heaven arena otherwise turned the whole screen black.
- **Dungeon hazard glow:** `glowHazards` in `hazard-glow.js` (called from `arena` in `world.js`) gives each instant-kill obstacle a cloned material with a pulsing rim glow and adds a glowing pool under it. Colours and strengths are in `HAZARD_GLOW`: red magma rock, green bushes, gold clouds (`tint` also gilds the cloud body). The pulse is driven by `doors.updateGlow(clock)` in `main.js`.
- **Motion blur:** `post-fx.js` renders the scene to an off-screen target with depth, reprojects each pixel with the previous frame's camera to get its screen motion, and blurs along it, so running streaks the world outward and strafing, turns and camera shake smear sideways. The runner (put on layer 1) is drawn into a mask and kept sharp. `main.js` sets the amount from the run pace only while running; elsewhere the blur passes are skipped. B toggles it (saved under `gmt-jam.blur`). Tune `SAMPLES` and `MAX_BLUR` in the module.
- **Turns:** the stage's `turns` list sets each turn by `after` (obstacle segments since the previous turn) and `angle` (degrees, positive is right). `layout` in `world.js` places a curved, hazard-free floor piece between two segments for each turn.
- **Speed:** runner speed ramps linearly from `speed` to `topSpeed` (both in `stage.js`) over the length of the route.
- **Jump and respawn feel:** `JUMP`, `RISE_GRAVITY`, `FALL_GRAVITY` and `JUMP_BUFFER` near `resetRunner` in `main.js` control the jump. The peak is `JUMP²/(2·RISE_GRAVITY)` (about 3.6) so rocks (`height<1.95`), gate walls (`<2.45`) and sliders (`SLIDE_H`, 2.7) can be jumped; high gravity keeps the airtime around 0.6 s. Fire jets cannot be jumped. Coin arcs over gaps and hurdles and the chests there sit near the jump's peak (`hop` and `safeY` in `world.js`). After a respawn the runner holds still for `RESPAWN_PAUSE`, then speeds back up from base `speed` over `RESPAWN_RAMP` seconds.
- **Embers and score:** `world.js` places rows of embers along each segment's safe line, a sidestep lane or the jump arc. `embers.chance` and `embers.value` in `stage.js` tune them. Score is 1 point per unit of new distance, plus `embers.value` × the streak multiplier per ember, close-call bonuses, and 500 for defeating the guardian. Embers and bonuses collected since the last checkpoint are taken back if the player dies.
- **Streak and close calls:** in `main.js`, each ember or close call raises `streak` and restarts a `STREAK_WINDOW` (3 s) timer; when it runs out or the player dies the streak resets. `multiplier()` is ×1 plus one step per 5 streak, up to ×5. A close call (`closeCall`) is passing a hazard without touching it but within a margin (rocks or fire within 3 units, a gate edge, a hurdle cleared low, a pendulum just missed); it is worth `NEAR_BONUS` × the multiplier and is detected next to the collision checks in `run`, using `h.close` and `h.passed` on each hazard. `#streak` (top centre) shows the multiplier with a draining timer bar. `worldPop` and `callout` add the floating numbers and big centre text to `#popups`; styles are under "Runner juice" in `style.css`.
- **Runner camera (third/first person):** V or the camera badge `#view-toggle` (bottom right, shown with the progress bar; styles under "Camera view badge" in `style.css`) toggles `firstPerson` (saved in `localStorage` under `gmt-jam.view`). `frameCamera` blends `runnerView` (chase cam) into `firstPersonView` (head height, stride bob) by `fpBlend`, and the player model is hidden once the camera is inside it. Nothing in the scene changes between the two views.
- **Runner juice:** `runner-juice.js` creates ribbon trails from the feet (flat light streaks on the floor) and hands (camera-facing, only at speed), footfall sparks, jump/land/pickup/death bursts and shockwave rings, coloured per realm by `PALETTES` (Heaven uses gold, normally blended trails because additive light washes out on its white floor). `juice.camera` runs after `camera.lookAt` and adds speed FOV, strafe roll, landing dip and trauma shake; `juice.scaleDt` applies hit-stop on death and a short slow motion on close calls. Trails are only emitted in third person. Speed lines (`#speedlines`) and the death flash (`#flash`) are DOM overlays. Sounds come from `sfx.js`, which starts on the first key or click.
- **Runner hazards:** the runner tracks the player in route space: `dist` along the path, `lat` to its right, and `height`. `world.js` builds each obstacle in its segment's local frame and records its `d` (distance along the route) and `x` (side offset). Its `at(dist, lat)` function converts route space to a world position and heading. The `run` function in `main.js` checks collisions in route space and animates fire and pendulums. When you add or resize an obstacle, update both files.
- **Guardian balance:** Edit health, damage, chase speed and attack order in `stage.js`. Edit attack range, wind-up and recovery in `combat.js`, alongside the floor indicators and hit detection.
- **Stage progression:** `main.js` loads the stage named `depths` when a run begins. To chain another stage using existing hazards and attacks, register its definition in `stage.js` and set the preceding stage's `next` to the new key. A null `next` leads to Heaven.
- **Controls:** Change action bindings in `input.js` and update the control reminders in `main.js`, the `#controls` panel in `index.html`, and `README.md`. The render loop consumes one-frame actions once per frame.
- **Pixel UI theme:** all HUD panels, buttons, keycaps, meters, popups, the pause list, the shop and the cutscene box follow the progress bar sprite: black pixel outlines with notched corners, a dark-red bevel, white keycaps/buttons with dark-red text, and pixel fonts. The rules are in the "Pixel UI theme" block at the end of `style.css`, which overrides the earlier smooth styles. `--u` is the UI pixel size and the palette variables are in its `:root`. Fonts come from npm (`@fontsource/pixelify-sans`, `@fontsource/silkscreen`) and are imported at the top of `main.js`.
- **Runner progress bar:** `public/sprites/finish_line.png` is shown at the top of the screen during the run. `drawProgress` in `main.js` grows a smooth dark-to-bright red gradient across the bar's interior in proportion to `dist / route.end`, and `showProgress` shows the bar only in the `run` and `fall` states. The gradient colours, the bar length (`--bar-length`) and the pixel size (`--px`, screen pixels per sprite pixel) are under "Runner progress bar" in `style.css`. The score sits in the top-left corner above the objective panel.
- **Runner controls panel:** `#controls` in `index.html` lists the runner keys in the top-right corner. Keys are drawn as keycaps. It shows and hides with the progress bar (`showProgress` in `main.js`); its styles are under "Runner controls panel" in `style.css`. Update it when you change the runner bindings in `input.js`.
- **Hearts and fight HUD:** player health is hearts. In `combat.js`, `hp` is white hearts (`MAX_HEARTS` = 3) and `overheal` is yellow hearts (up to `MAX_OVERHEAL` = 2). `damagePlayer` costs one heart per hit, yellow first; `heal` (a potion, `POTION_HEARTS` = 2) repairs broken hearts first and turns the rest into overheal. The Vitality Elixir in the shop sets `progress.overheal`, which is carried into the next fight. `drawFightHud` in `main.js` draws the hearts from `public/sprites/hearts.png` under the score, the wave counter at the top, and the dodge (cyan) and special (purple) bars under the player. Styles are under "Fight HUD" in `style.css`.
- **Pause menu sections:** `SECTIONS` in `main.js` lists every stage's runner and combat section in order. `clearSection` marks one finished (runner: reaching the gate; combat: clearing the chamber) and saves the set in `localStorage` under `gmt-jam.sections`, so it survives the end of a run and page reloads. `renderSections` draws the list in the pause menu: locked sections grey, finished ones white, the current one orange. Clicking a white section calls `revisit`, which loads that stage and starts its runner or its fight. Styles are under "Pause menu" in `style.css`. To reset progress, clear that localStorage key.
- **Timed runner hazard (`fire`):** `lava-vents.js` draws it. In Hell, `lavaJet` is a lava pool that gurgles while safe, bubbles hard as a warning, then erupts in a churning column that sprays blobs. In Heaven, `goldenGate` is a faint flickering gate while safe that turns solid gold while deadly. Both follow the same 3.5 s cycle as the collision check in `run` (`WARN_AT` 1.5 s, `ERUPT_AT` 2.2 s), so changing the timing means changing both. Stages that are neither infernal nor celestial keep the plain orange block.
- **Damage popups:** `reportHit` in `combat.js` records every hit on an enemy or the boss (position above its head, damage, and whether it was heavy: a combo finisher, a special, a slash wave or a reflected fireball). `showHits` in `main.js` projects each one to the screen and adds a popup with `public/sprites/red_nacho.png` and the number. Size, colours and animation are under "Damage popups" in `style.css`.
- **Opening cutscene:** two full-screen animated scenes (`angel_drop`, then `don_quixote`) at 4 fps, with dialogue over them. Write lines in `script` in `dialogue.js`; each has `scene` (a key in `scenes`), `speaker` (name tag, or null for narration) and `text`. To add a scene, put a sheet of square frames stacked vertically in `public/sprites/` and add it to `scenes` with its `fps`. The frame count comes from the sheet's height. Styling is under "Visual-novel opening cutscene" in `style.css`.

## Run locally

Open a terminal in the directory containing `package.json`. With Node.js 22.12 or newer, run `npm ci` and then `npm run dev`. Open the local URL Vite prints. For static hosting, run `npm run build` and serve the generated `dist/` directory.
